What Actually Happens in Your First Tennis Lesson

By Coach Tommy O'Donnell October 6, 2026 4 min read

Almost every first phone call I get starts the same way. Somebody tells me they're not very good.

Sometimes it's an adult who hasn't held a racquet since high school. Sometimes it's a parent apologizing in advance for a seven-year-old who has never hit a ball. Either way, the worry is the same: what if I show up and I'm the worst one there?

So here's how it works. This is what happens the first time we get on the court.

You don't need to be good yet

This is the part I most want you to hear. A first lesson isn't a tryout. Nobody is grading you.

I've been coaching since 1985. And beginners are some of my favorite people to teach. There are no bad habits to undo, so we get to build good ones from the start.

If you can walk onto a court, you're ready.

What to bring

Less than you think.

👟

Court shoes

Tennis or court shoes if you have them. Running shoes grip wrong for side-to-side movement.

💧

Water

More than you'd guess. This is Florida.

🧢

Sun cover

A hat, sunscreen, and clothes you can move in.

🎾

A racquet, maybe

Don't buy one yet. Ask me first so you get the right size.

That last one matters. A lot of parents buy a racquet before the first lesson and get the wrong size. If it's too long or too heavy for a kid, tennis gets harder than it needs to be. So text me first. I'll tell you what to get, or whether to wait.

The first few minutes: we talk

Before we hit anything, I want to know why you're here.

Do you want to play doubles with friends? Make the high school team? Get some exercise that doesn't feel like exercise? Is your kid there because they asked, or because you're hoping they'll like it?

None of those are wrong answers. But they lead to different lessons. I'd rather know where we're headed than guess.

Then you hit, and I watch

We'll start close to the net with easy balls. No pressure, no score.

While you're hitting, I'm not looking for mistakes. I'm looking at what you already do on your own. How you move. Where you hold the racquet when nobody's told you how. Whether you watch the ball or the person across from you.

That tells me more than any question could. It's how I figure out where to start with you. I don't teach from a script, because what works for one beginner usually isn't what the next one needs.

We work on one thing

Not ten. One.

Maybe it's how you hold the racquet. Maybe it's turning your shoulders before you swing. Maybe it's keeping your eyes on the ball a half second longer. We pick the one thing that will help you most right now, and we stay on it.

Here's the thing. Give a new player ten corrections and they leave confused. Give them one and they usually leave hitting better than when they walked in.

For Parents

You're welcome to stay and watch. The best thing you can do from the bench is smile and stay quiet. Kids relax faster when the lesson feels like theirs. And relaxed kids learn faster.

We finish with something fun

Especially with juniors, the last part of a lesson is a game. A rally challenge, a target, a point or two.

That isn't filler. Whether someone comes back for lesson two mostly comes down to whether lesson one was fun. I want new players walking off the court wanting to hit again.

What you leave with

Before you go, I'll tell you what I saw and what we'll work on next time. I'll also give you one simple thing to practice on your own, if you want it.

And if you think of questions after you get home, call or text me. That's part of the lesson too.

The hardest part is booking it

I mean that. A lot of people who call me have been thinking about it for months.

The court is the easy part. Come as you are, bring some water, and we'll take it from there.
